The Structure of a Great Article

A B1 level article needs to be fun to read! Imagine you are writing for a school magazine. Here is how you build it:

  • 1. A Catchy Title: Grab the reader's attention immediately. Make it interesting! (e.g., The Best Day Ever! instead of A Day at the Park).
  • 2. The Introduction: Hook the reader. Start with a question directed at them (e.g., Have you ever tried...? or Are you looking for a great hobby?).
  • 3. The Body Paragraph(s): This is where you answer the questions from the exam prompt. Use lots of description and give your personal opinion. Make sure to talk about your own experiences!
  • 4. The Conclusion: Wrap it up nicely. Give a final piece of advice or a strong closing thought (e.g., If you get the chance, you should definitely try it!).

Essential Vocabulary & Linking Words

To get a high score, you need to connect your ideas smoothly and use exciting adjectives instead of boring ones.

Linking Words

  • To add ideas: Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Also.
  • To show contrast: However, Although, On the other hand, Even though.
  • To give reasons: Because, Since, Therefore, As a result, So.
  • To sequence: First of all, Next, Then, Finally, To sum up.

Giving Opinions

  • In my opinion,...
  • To be honest,...
  • Personally, I think that...

B1 Adjectives (Avoid "good" or "bad")

Try using words like: Fascinating, Exhausting, Thrilling, Terrifying, Amazing, Unforgettable.

Exam Checklist: Minimum Requirements

Before you finish your exam, check that your article has all of these:

  • Word Count: You must write about 100 words. Don't write too little (under 80) or way too much (over 130).
  • Answer the Prompt: Did you answer ALL the questions asked in the magazine announcement? If the prompt asks "What is your favorite film and why?", you must include the "why".
  • Appropriate Tone: Your article should be informal and friendly. Do not write it like a formal essay or a letter to a boss.
  • B1 Grammar: Try to show off your B1 skills! Use a mix of tenses (Present Perfect, Past Simple), comparative adjectives, and maybe a conditional sentence (If I have time, I will go again!).
